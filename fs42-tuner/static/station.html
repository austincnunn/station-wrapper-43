What plays, and when

Draw slots on the week: drag on a day to make one, drag its top or bottom edge to resize it, drag the slot itself to move it (to another time or another day), click it to choose what plays, press Delete to remove the selected slot, and use Ctrl+C, Ctrl+X and Ctrl+V to copy, cut and paste it (it pastes where the pointer is). Time you leave unassigned plays the test pattern, so a week can be built up a little at a time. Seasons change the whole week for part of the year; special days change just the days (or slots) you choose.

The normal week. It plays whenever no season or special day says otherwise.

Details

Programs start on these marks, and breaks fill the gap to the next one. Pick 5 for a movie channel, so a movie is rounded up to the next 5 minutes instead of the next half hour.

A show rarely fills its slot exactly: a 22 minute show in a 30 minute slot leaves 8 minutes to fill. This chooses where that time goes. Inside the show uses the chapter marks in the video, or its black frames, and falls back to spreading the breaks out if it has neither. A show that fills its slot gets no break at all.

A video that fits more than once into the slot shares it: with 30 minute slots an 11 minute video gets a 15 minute block (two to the half hour), a 9 minute one gets 10 (three), under 7.5 gets four, and 1 minute clips run back to back. Only the time left over in each block is filled with commercials. Off, every video gets a whole slot, however short.

If a video has an .edl file beside it (as Comskip writes), its breaks are used. Otherwise, for videos whose chapter marks are not exact: when the catalog is built or updated, each mark is moved to the nearest black frame or silence (up to 90 seconds away); a mark with none nearby stays where it is. It reads each video once, so the first build takes longer.

Off (the default for a new station): a break is commercials only, with no card before or after, except where you gave cards to a time of day (the calendar's Mid cards tab) or to a show (below): choosing cards there is the opt in for those videos. On: the two lists below play at every break that has none of its own.

Default start-of-break mid cards

Short clips (a few seconds, like a "we'll be right back") played at the start of each commercial break. They are only used when breaks with commercials are on.

Default end-of-break mid cards

Short clips (like a "welcome back") played at the end of each break, just before the show returns. It can be the same list as the start cards.

Off (the default for a new station): there are no breaks. Videos play back to back, the next one starting as soon as one ends, and the time slots are only a soft target that the day is filled to with videos from the lists, except in hours where you gave the calendar's Commercials tab a list: choosing one there is the opt in for those hours. On: the spare time in each slot is filled with clips from the list chosen here, at the breaks set above, in every hour that has none of its own.

Plays

A list, or a series, a season, a folder, a video or a YouTube playlist: add as many as you like, and they play as one.

The guide channel

A scrolling grid of what is on every channel, drawn live (the classic TV guide channel). Nothing needs scheduling or building: it is on air as soon as it is saved.

Standard draws the guide in the middle of the picture with black bars at the sides, like any 4:3 programme: a 4:3 TV that crops the sides shows all of it, edge to edge.

Bigger text is easier to read on a small or old TV, with fewer channels on screen at once (the rest scroll up). Showing fewer hours gives each programme more room.

The channels scroll up when there are more than fit on the screen, and the messages scroll along the bottom.

Lines like "Coming up at 9:00 PM: Friends on Channel 117", picked from the guide's own listings for the next few hours. Picked again every hour, after your own messages.

Small, after the time in this guide (8:00 – 8:30 PM · The Pilot); a YouTube video shows its channel's name big and the video's title small. Anywhere that has one line (the banner, the schedule, other guides) reads "Friends: The Pilot". Off, only the show's or channel's name is shown. This is one setting for the whole system, kept when you save this page. Every name too long for its slot scrolls.

Pay-per-view

A menu of movies on your TV players: page through the posters with the remote's up and down, and press Enter to watch one from the start. Each player draws it itself (in client mode), so it is not in Emby or Jellyfin. Titles, plots and posters come from your media server when it knows the movie, otherwise from the folder names. New movies show up within half an hour.

Every video in it and in the folders inside it, one per movie.

Saving only keeps your choices. Building makes the catalog and a new week of schedule from them; if the station already has a schedule, it keeps playing until the new one is ready. Changes to seasons or special days are used for days added from now on, so build when you want them to apply to the days already scheduled.
